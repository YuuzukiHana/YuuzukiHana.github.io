Protein Expression and Analysis

Protocol ID: P-LLO-02
Prepared by: TDY
Reviewed by: TDY
Version: 2026-10-02
Status: Draft
Related results: Western blot of LLO

Purpose

To induce LLO expression in yeast, extract total yeast protein, determine protein concentration, and detect the target protein by western blotting.

1. Protein Expression Induction

  1. Transfer 1 mL of the culture into 10 mL of fresh SD-Ura + 2% raffinose medium without glucose.
  2. Incubate for 1 day.
  3. Transfer 1 mL of the culture into 10 mL of fresh SD-Ura-YPG medium.
  4. Incubate for 1 day.
  5. Harvest the yeast and proceed with downstream testing.

2. Protein Extraction

Use the BestBio Yeast Protein Extraction Kit, Cat. No. BB-319817-50T.[1]

2.1 Preparation of Extraction Buffer

Add 2 μL of protease inhibitor mixture to every 500 μL of yeast protein extraction buffer B. Mix thoroughly and keep the buffer on ice until use.

2.2 Extraction Procedure

  1. Harvest the yeast culture by centrifugation at 2,500 × g for 5–10 min at 4°C.
  2. Carefully remove the supernatant while removing as much culture medium as possible. Retain the yeast pellet.
  3. Wash the yeast pellet twice with cold PBS. Remove the supernatant completely after each wash.
  4. Resuspend the yeast pellet in 300–500 μL of cold yeast protein extraction buffer A. Use 100–200 μL of buffer per original pellet volume, as appropriate.
  5. Mix thoroughly and incubate with shaking at room temperature or 37°C for 1–2 h.
  6. Centrifuge at 2,000 × g for 5–10 min at 4°C.
  7. Remove the supernatant and retain the pellet.
  8. Resuspend the pellet in 300–500 μL of cold yeast protein extraction buffer B.
  9. Mix thoroughly and incubate with gentle shaking at 2–8°C for 30–45 min.
  10. Centrifuge at 12,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C.
  11. Transfer the supernatant to a pre-chilled, clean centrifuge tube. This supernatant is the total yeast protein extract.
  12. Quantify the protein extract, aliquot it, and store it at −80°C for future use, or proceed directly to downstream experiments.

3. Protein Quantification

Use the Beyotime BCA Protein Assay Kit, Cat. No. P0012.[2]

3.1 Preparation of the Protein Standard

  1. Add 1.2 mL of protein standard solution to a tube containing 30 mg of BSA standard. Mix thoroughly to prepare a 25 mg/mL protein standard solution.
  2. The 25 mg/mL standard may be used immediately or stored at −20°C for long-term use.
  3. Dilute the 25 mg/mL standard to 0.5 mg/mL. For example, mix 20 μL of the 25 mg/mL standard with 980 μL of diluent.
  4. Use the same solution to dilute the samples and the standards whenever possible. The standard may also be diluted with 0.9% NaCl or PBS.
  5. The diluted 0.5 mg/mL standard may be stored at −20°C.

3.2 Preparation of the BCA Working Solution

Prepare the BCA working solution by mixing reagent A and reagent B at a ratio of 50:1.

For example, mix 5 mL of BCA reagent A with 100 μL of BCA reagent B. The working solution is stable at room temperature for up to 24 h.

3.3 Standard Curve

Add the indicated volume of 0.5 mg/mL protein standard to the standard wells. Add standard diluent to a final volume of 20 μL per well.

4.2 SDS-PAGE Loading and Electrophoresis

  1. Mix each protein sample with SDS-PAGE loading buffer.
  2. Heat the samples at 95–100°C for 5–10 min.
  3. Centrifuge briefly at 12,000 rpm.
  4. Load approximately 3–4 μL of prestained protein marker.
  5. Load equal amounts of protein into the gel wells. A typical loading volume is 15–20 μL per well.
  6. Perform electrophoresis at 150 V for approximately 40–50 min.
  7. Remove the gel after electrophoresis and proceed with protein transfer.

4.3 Protein Transfer

  1. Prepare the transfer buffer by dissolving one packet of transfer buffer powder in 700 mL of water.

  2. Add 200 mL of methanol and bring the final volume to 1 L with water.

  3. Pre-cool the transfer buffer to 4°C.

  4. Activate the PVDF membrane in methanol for 1–2 min.

  5. Equilibrate the membrane in transfer buffer.

  6. Assemble the transfer sandwich in the following orientation:

    Cathode → sponge → filter paper → gel → PVDF membrane → filter paper → sponge → anode

  7. Carefully remove air bubbles between the gel and membrane.

  8. Place the transfer sandwich in the transfer tank containing pre-cooled transfer buffer.

  9. Transfer proteins at a constant current of 230 mA for 1.5 h.

  10. Place ice packs in the transfer tank and surround the tank with ice during transfer.

4.4 Membrane Blocking

  1. Prepare blocking solution by dissolving 2.5 g of skimmed milk powder in 500 mL of TBST.
  2. After transfer, place the PVDF membrane in an incubation box with the protein-facing side upward.
  3. Incubate the membrane in blocking solution at room temperature for 60 min.
  4. Agitate gently on a rocking platform at 40–60 rpm.

4.5 Primary Antibody Incubation

  1. Rinse the membrane once with TBST for 5 min on a rocking platform.
  2. Cut the membrane according to the expected molecular-weight range of the target protein.
  3. Dilute the primary antibody in blocking solution.
  4. Use a general working dilution of 1:1,000. Prepare tag-specific antibodies at approximately 1:2,500 unless otherwise specified.
  5. Incubate the membrane with the primary antibody at 4°C overnight.

4.6 Secondary Antibody Incubation

  1. Remove and retain the primary antibody solution for possible reuse. Retained primary antibody may be reused up to three times or for one week.
  2. Wash the membrane three times with TBST at room temperature. Each wash should last 5 min with agitation at 40–60 rpm.
  3. Dilute the appropriate HRP-conjugated secondary antibody in blocking solution at approximately 1:2,500.
  4. Incubate the membrane with the secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature with gentle agitation and protection from light.
  5. Remove the secondary antibody solution. Do not retain the secondary antibody solution.

4.7 Chemiluminescence Detection

  1. Wash the membrane three times with TBST at room temperature. Each wash should last 5 min.
  2. During the final washing steps, start the chemiluminescence imaging system and adjust the imaging parameters.
  3. Incubate the membrane with the Millipore WBKLS0500 chemiluminescence detection reagent.
  4. Use an exposure time between 0.5 and 2 s to capture the signal.

Tetrahymena Co-incubation

Protocol ID: P-LLO-03
Prepared by: TDY
Reviewed by: TDY
Version: 2026-10-02
Status: Draft
Related results: Tetrahymena mortality assay; Tetrahymena ACP activity assay

Materials

Biological Materials

  • Stationary-phase Tetrahymena cultures
  • Stationary-phase yeast expressing LLO
  • Stationary-phase wild-type yeast

Reagents

  • SPP medium
  • Sterile 10 mM Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.4–7.5
  • Trypan blue solution

Part I. Starvation of Tetrahymena

1. Collection of Tetrahymena

  1. Gently mix the Tetrahymena culture to obtain a homogeneous suspension.
  2. Transfer the required volume into sterile centrifuge tubes.
  3. Balance the centrifuge tubes carefully.
  4. Centrifuge at 1,100 × g for 2–3 min at room temperature.
  5. Carefully remove the SPP supernatant without disturbing the cell pellet.
  6. Avoid excessive centrifugation speed or prolonged centrifugation, as either may cause cell damage or lysis.

2. Washing of Tetrahymena

  1. Add sterile 10 mM Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.4–7.5, at a volume equal to the original culture volume.
  2. Gently resuspend the pellet by slow pipetting.
  3. Avoid vigorous pipetting, vortexing, and foaming.
  4. Centrifuge at 1,100 × g for 2–3 min at room temperature.
  5. Carefully remove the supernatant.
  6. Repeat the washing procedure for a total of three washes.
  7. Resuspend the final Tetrahymena pellet in sterile 10 mM Tris-HCl buffer.

3. Starvation Incubation

  1. Transfer the washed Tetrahymena suspension into a sterile culture tube or bottle.
  2. Leave sufficient headspace for gas exchange.
  3. Keep the container loosely capped.
  4. Incubate the washed cells at 30°C for 12 h.

The starvation period is intended to reduce pre-existing food vacuoles and improve subsequent yeast uptake.

Part II. Preparation of Yeast Cultures

4. Preparation of the Yeast Groups

Prepare the following stationary-phase yeast cultures:

  • Yeast expressing LLO
  • Wild-type yeast

Dilute the yeast cultures with liquid medium to approximately the intended cell concentration. Gently resuspend each yeast culture immediately before use.

Part III. Co-incubation of Tetrahymena with Yeast

5. Distribution of Cultures

Prepare the following groups. Each group has a final volume of 12 mL.

Group Culture condition Tetrahymena culture Yeast culture Final volume
Group 1 Tetrahymena alone 12 mL 0 mL 12 mL
Group 2 Tetrahymena with wild-type yeast 6 mL 6 mL wild-type yeast 12 mL
Group 3 Tetrahymena with LLO-expressing yeast 6 mL 6 mL LLO-expressing yeast 12 mL
  1. Label the tubes according to group.
  2. Add the indicated culture volumes.
  3. Gently mix each tube by slowly inverting it several times.

6. Co-incubation

  1. Keep the tube caps loose to allow gas exchange.
  2. Incubate all groups at 30°C for 24 h.
  3. Avoid opening the tubes unnecessarily during incubation.

Part IV. Trypan Blue Staining and Hemocytometer Counting

7. Light Microscopy

  1. Gently mix each 24-h co-incubation culture.

  2. Mix the Tetrahymena sample with trypan blue at a 1:1 ratio without creating bubbles.

  3. Load the stained sample into a clean hemocytometer with the coverslip in place.

  4. Examine the hemocytometer under a light microscope.

  5. Count the Tetrahymena cells in the designated counting areas.

  6. Record unstained and stained cells separately:

    • Unstained, structurally intact cells: viable cells
    • Blue-stained or visibly damaged cells: non-viable or damaged cells

  7. Avoid counting yeast cells as Tetrahymena.

  8. Use the same counting pattern for all samples.

  9. Record the cell counts for each group.

8. Calculation of Tetrahymena Viability

Calculate viability using:

$$
\text{Viability (%)} =
\frac{N_{\text{unstained}}}
{N_{\text{unstained}} + N_{\text{blue or damaged}}}
\times 100%
$$

Compare the following parameters among the groups:

  • Remaining Tetrahymena concentration
  • Number of viable cells
  • Number of stained or damaged cells
  • Percentage of viable cells
  • Cell morphology
  • Motility

Part V. Partial Lysis of Tetrahymena

1. Preparation of Tetrahymena Imaging Buffer

Tetrahymena imaging buffer (TIB) was adapted from the extracellular solution used for electrophysiological studies of Tetrahymena by Hennessey and Kung (1984).[3]

Prepare 1 L of TIB as follows:

Component Final concentration Amount required for 1 L
Sucrose 100 mM 34.23 g
HEPES 10 mM 10 mL of 1 M HEPES stock, pH 8.0
NaCl 50 mM 2.92 g
KCl 5 mM 0.373 g
CaCl₂·2H₂O 1 mM 0.147 g
MgCl₂·6H₂O 1 mM 0.203 g
  1. Add the components to approximately 800–900 mL of ultrapure water.
  2. Mix until all components have dissolved.
  3. Adjust the pH to 7.2 ± 0.1.
  4. Bring the final volume to 1 L with ultrapure water.
  5. Mix thoroughly.
  6. Pre-cool the TIB to 4°C before cell washing and lysis.
  7. Keep the TIB on ice before use.

2. Preparation of 0.1% Triton X-100 in TIB

Prepare the Triton-containing lysis solution before starting the experiment.

For 10 mL of 0.1% Triton X-100/TIB:

Component Amount
Triton X-100 10 μL
TIB To 10 mL
  1. Mix thoroughly.
  2. Pre-cool the solution to 4°C.
  3. Keep it on ice before use.
  4. Do not add concentrated Triton X-100 directly to the cell pellet.
  5. Do not add EDTA or phosphatase inhibitors.

3. Experimental Groups and Controls

Group Experimental condition Fraction analyzed
Group 1 Tetrahymena alone G1S20 and G1P20
Group 2 Tetrahymena co-incubated with wild-type yeast G2S20 and G2P20
Group 3 Tetrahymena co-incubated with LLO-expressing yeast G3S20 and G3P20

Use the corresponding co-incubated cultures prepared above.

4. Low-Speed Collection of Tetrahymena

  1. Transfer the co-incubation sample to a pre-chilled 50 mL centrifuge tube.
  2. Immediately place the sample on ice.
  3. Centrifuge at 100 × g for 60 s at 4°C.
  4. Use low brake or no brake if available.
  5. Carefully aspirate the supernatant while leaving approximately 0.5 mL above the pellet.
  6. Do not touch or disturb the pellet.

5. Washing with TIB

Perform two washes with ice-cold TIB.

First Wash
  1. Add 20 mL of ice-cold TIB to the pellet.
  2. Gently resuspend the cells with a wide-bore pipette tip.
  3. Pipette slowly approximately three times.
  4. Do not vortex.
  5. Centrifuge at 100 × g for 60 s at 4°C using low brake or no brake.
  6. Carefully remove the supernatant while leaving the pellet undisturbed.
Second Wash
  1. Add another 20 mL of ice-cold TIB.
  2. Gently resuspend the pellet with a wide-bore pipette tip.
  3. Centrifuge again at 100 × g for 60 s at 4°C.
  4. Carefully remove the supernatant.
  5. Retain the final Tetrahymena pellet for Triton lysis.

If necessary, inspect a small aliquot microscopically after the second wash to assess extracellular yeast carryover. Do not increase the centrifugation speed to remove residual yeast, because this may cause free yeast to co-pellet with Tetrahymena.

6. Resuspension and Triton Lysis

  1. Use the pre-cooled 0.1% Triton X-100/TIB solution.
  2. Remove as much residual TIB as possible without disturbing the pellet. The remaining supernatant should be less than 0.5 mL.
  3. Add exactly 1.00 mL of pre-cooled 0.1% Triton X-100/TIB directly to the pellet.
  4. Keep the tube on ice.
  5. Gently resuspend the pellet using a wide-bore pipette tip.
  6. Do not vortex.
  7. Incubate the suspension on ice for 2 min.
  8. After 2 min, immediately add 10 mL of TIB to dilute the lysate and stop lysis.

7. Separation of S20 and P20 Fractions

  1. Centrifuge at 20,000 × g for 15 min at 4°C.

  2. After centrifugation, the sample contains:

    • S20: supernatant
    • P20: pellet

  3. Process all samples consistently and avoid disturbing the P20 pellet.

S20 Supernatant
  1. Carefully transfer the supernatant to a labeled, pre-chilled tube.
  2. Avoid contact with the pellet.
  3. Record the recovered S20 volume.
  4. Keep the S20 sample on ice.
  5. The S20 sample may be used directly as the ACP assay test solution, subject to any dilution required by the kit.
P20 Pellet
  1. Leave the P20 pellet in the original centrifuge tube.
  2. Keep the tube on ice.
  3. Add 200 μL of ACP extraction buffer from the ACMEC biochemical ACP Activity Assay Kit and half a spoon of 0.1 mm glass beads.
  4. Vortex for 30 s.
  5. Cool on ice for 30 s.
  6. Repeat the vortexing and cooling cycle four times.
  7. Centrifuge at 10,000 × g for 1 min at 4°C.
  8. Transfer the supernatant to a new pre-chilled tube.
  9. Use this solution for the ACP activity assay.

Construction of pYES2-LLO and Transformation into Yeast

Protocol ID: P-LLO-04
Prepared by: TDY
Reviewed by: TDY
Version: 2026-10-02
Status: Draft

Purpose

To construct the recombinant plasmid pYES2-LLO by directionally inserting the LLO DNA fragment into the BamHI/XbaI sites of pYES2, followed by transformation of the ligation product into competent yeast cells and selection on SD-Ura agar.

Before beginning, confirm that the LLO fragment does not contain internal BamHI or XbaI sites and that its orientation, start and stop codons, and reading frame are compatible with the intended pYES2 expression design.

Materials

DNA and Enzymes

  • pYES2 plasmid DNA
  • LLO plasmid DNA
  • BamHI-HF restriction enzyme
  • XbaI restriction enzyme
  • 10× rCutSmart™ Buffer
  • T4 DNA Ligase, NEB M0202
  • 10× T4 DNA Ligase Buffer
  • Nuclease-free water

DNA Purification and Electrophoresis

  • Agarose
  • 1× TAE electrophoresis buffer
  • DNA loading buffer
  • DNA ladder
  • GelRed
  • GeneBetter Gel Extraction Kit, Cat. No. DC013-200
  • NanoDrop spectrophotometer
  • Agarose gel electrophoresis apparatus
  • Gel imaging system
  • Blue-light transilluminator, when available

Yeast Transformation

  • W303 Saccharomyces cerevisiae
  • Beyotime Yeast Competent Cell Preparation Kit, Cat. No. D0310S
  • YPD medium
  • SD-Ura selective agar plates

Before Starting

  1. Confirm the expected sizes of the pYES2 backbone and LLO insert.
  2. Confirm that the LLO fragment contains BamHI- and XbaI-compatible ends.
  3. Confirm that the LLO sequence does not contain unwanted internal BamHI or XbaI sites.
  4. Confirm that the yeast strain is compatible with URA3 selection.
  5. SD-Ura selection will not work correctly with a uracil-prototrophic strain.

Part I. Double Digestion of pYES2 and the LLO Insert

Prepare separate restriction-digestion reactions for the pYES2 vector and the LLO insert.

1. Digestion Reaction Setup

Prepare each reaction in a final volume of 50 μL.

Component pYES2 digestion LLO digestion
pYES2 DNA Approximately 1 μg —
LLO DNA — Approximately 1 μg
10× rCutSmart™ Buffer 5 μL 5 μL
BamHI-HF 1 μL, approximately 20 U 1 μL, approximately 20 U
XbaI 1 μL, approximately 20 U 1 μL, approximately 20 U
Nuclease-free water To 50 μL To 50 μL
Total volume 50 μL 50 μL
  1. Thaw the buffer and DNA samples and mix thoroughly.
  2. Assemble the reactions on ice.
  3. Add the restriction enzymes last.
  4. Mix gently by pipetting. Do not vortex the enzymes.
  5. Briefly centrifuge the tubes to collect the liquid at the bottom.
  6. Incubate at 37°C for 15–60 min.
  7. Keep the digested DNA on ice until gel electrophoresis.
  8. Run a small aliquot of undigested pYES2 alongside the digested sample when needed to assess digestion efficiency.

Part II. Agarose Gel Electrophoresis and DNA Recovery

  1. Prepare a 0.5% agarose gel using 1× TAE buffer and GelRed.

  2. Add loading dye to the digestion products.

  3. Load the samples together with a suitable DNA ladder.

  4. Run the gel until the linearized pYES2 backbone and LLO insert can be distinguished clearly.

  5. Visualize the DNA with a blue-light transilluminator whenever possible.

  6. If UV illumination is used, minimize exposure time to reduce DNA damage.

  7. Excise the following bands:

    • Linearized pYES2 backbone
    • Digested LLO insert

  8. Minimize the amount of excess agarose surrounding each band.

  9. Purify both DNA fragments using the GeneBetter DC013-200 Gel Extraction Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

  10. Measure the concentration of the recovered DNA using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer.

A 0.5% gel is suitable for resolving large DNA fragments but is relatively fragile. If the LLO fragment is approximately 1–2 kb and the resolution is poor, use a 0.8–1.0% agarose gel.

Part III. Ligation of the LLO Insert into pYES2

Use an intended vector-to-insert molar ratio of 1:3.

Calculate the required insert mass using:

$$
m_{\text{insert}} =
m_{\text{vector}}
\times
\frac{L_{\text{insert}}}{L_{\text{vector}}}
\times
\text{molar ratio}
$$

where:

  • $m_{\text{insert}}$ is the required insert mass;
  • $m_{\text{vector}}$ is the vector mass;
  • $L_{\text{insert}}$ is the insert length;
  • $L_{\text{vector}}$ is the vector length;
  • the intended molar ratio is 3 for a 1:3 vector-to-insert ratio.

Confirm the ratio using the actual sizes and concentrations of the purified pYES2 backbone and LLO insert.

1. Ligation Reaction Setup

Prepare the following 20 μL reaction:

Component Amount
Linearized pYES2 vector Approximately 72.5 ng
Digested LLO insert Approximately 60 ng
10× T4 DNA Ligase Buffer 2 μL
T4 DNA Ligase, NEB M0202 1 μL, approximately 400 U
Nuclease-free water To 20 μL
Total volume 20 μL
  1. Thaw the T4 DNA Ligase Buffer at room temperature.
  2. Mix the buffer thoroughly to ensure that all components, including ATP, are evenly distributed.
  3. Add the vector, insert, ligase buffer, and nuclease-free water to a sterile tube.
  4. Add T4 DNA ligase last.
  5. Mix gently by pipetting and briefly centrifuge.
  6. Incubate at 16°C for 12–16 h.
  7. Heat-inactivate the ligase at 65°C for 10 min.
  8. Cool the reaction on ice and briefly centrifuge before transformation.

Part IV. Preparation of Yeast Competent Cells

  1. Start with a fresh colony or actively growing culture of the selected yeast strain.
  2. Prepare yeast competent cells using the Beyotime Yeast Competent Cell Preparation Kit, Cat. No. D0310S.
  3. When the OD600 of the freshly cultured yeast reaches 0.4–0.6, centrifuge at 3,000 × g for 3 min at room temperature.
  4. Discard the supernatant and retain the cell pellet.
  5. Resuspend the pellet in 7 mL of Buffer A.
  6. Centrifuge again at 3,000 × g for 3 min at room temperature.
  7. Discard the supernatant and retain the pellet.
  8. Resuspend the pellet in 400 μL of Buffer B.
  9. Incubate at room temperature for 10 min.
  10. Aliquot the prepared competent cells into sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes at 100 μL per tube.

Prepared competent cells should ideally be used immediately. They may be stored at 4°C for up to 1 week, although transformation efficiency may decrease over time. Alternatively, add glycerol to a final concentration of 15%, mix thoroughly, and store at −80°C for up to 3 months.

Part V. Transformation of the Ligation Product into Yeast

  1. Transfer an appropriate aliquot of prepared competent yeast cells into a sterile transformation tube.
  2. Mix 0.1–1 μg of plasmid DNA with 10 μL of heat-denatured carrier DNA by gentle pipetting.
  3. Add the DNA mixture to 100 μL of competent cells and gently tap to mix.
  4. Add 600 μL of Buffer C and invert gently to mix.
  5. Incubate in a 30°C water bath for 30 min.
  6. Add 70 μL of DMSO.
  7. Gently invert to mix.
  8. Incubate in a 42°C water bath for 15 min.
  9. Immediately transfer the tube to an ice bath and incubate for 3–5 min.
  10. Centrifuge at 12,000–14,000 × g for 10 s at room temperature.
  11. Discard the supernatant.
  12. Resuspend the pellet in 100 μL of Buffer A.
  13. Spread the entire suspension on an appropriate selective agar plate.
  14. Incubate the plate upside down at 30°C for 2–3 days until individual colonies appear.

Avoid unnecessary delays after competent-cell preparation.

Tetrahymena Culture, Passaging, Counting, and Standardization

Protocol ID: P-LLO-05
Prepared by: TDY
Reviewed by: TDY
Version: 2026-10-02
Status: Draft

1. Tetrahymena Culture

1.1 Purpose

To establish and maintain Tetrahymena cultures in SPP medium and monitor culture condition, cultivation time, and cell density under consistent conditions.

1.2 Materials

  • Tetrahymena stock culture
  • SPP medium: 10 g/L yeast extract, 1 g/L peptone, and 2 g/L glucose
  • Balance, weighing containers, mixing vessel, and volumetric measuring equipment
  • Sterile culture vessels, pipettes, pipette tips, and sampling tubes
  • Temperature-controlled incubation equipment
  • Shaker, if used
  • Light microscope, slides, coverslips, and calibrated counting chamber
  • Culture record sheet or electronic laboratory notebook

1.3 Procedure

  1. Confirm the identity of the stock culture. Record the species, strain, source, passage number if available, and date of the previous transfer.
  2. Prepare SPP medium. For 1 L of final medium, dissolve 10 g of yeast extract, 1 g of peptone, and 2 g of glucose in purified water. Bring the final volume to 1 L.
  3. Sterilize and store the medium according to the established laboratory procedure.
  4. Record the preparation date and batch identifier.
  5. Allow the medium to reach the inoculation temperature before use.
  6. Label the culture vessel with the strain, culture identifier, passage number, medium batch, and inoculation date and time.
  7. Gently mix the source culture and examine a separate aliquot microscopically.
  8. Record cell appearance, motility, aggregation, and any unexpected organisms or material.
  9. Determine the source-culture density using the laboratory counting method.
  10. If inoculation is density-based, calculate the volume required to obtain the intended starting density in the final working volume.
  11. If a transfer ratio is used, state explicitly whether it refers to inoculum:fresh medium or inoculum:final culture volume.
  12. Aseptically add fresh SPP medium and the calculated inoculum to the labeled vessel.
  13. Include the inoculum volume when calculating the final working volume.
  14. Mix gently.
  15. Incubate under the recorded temperature and static or shaking conditions.
  16. Record the incubation start time.
  17. At each scheduled observation, gently redistribute the suspension before sampling.
  18. Examine a separate aliquot microscopically and determine cell density.
  19. Record the sampling time and volume.
  20. Follow the documented density range and passage criterion.
  21. Document every transfer to fresh medium together with the density and age of the source culture.
  22. Complete the culture record with the incubation duration, final density, observed culture condition, and identifier of any derived culture.

2. Tetrahymena Passaging and Culture Expansion

2.1 Purpose

To transfer an established Tetrahymena culture into fresh medium for routine maintenance or to obtain a larger working culture while retaining a traceable passage history.

2.2 Materials

  • Tetrahymena source culture of the documented species and strain
  • Fresh sterile SPP medium: 10 g/L yeast extract, 1 g/L peptone, and 2 g/L glucose
  • Sterile receiving culture vessels, pipettes, pipette tips, and sampling tubes
  • Temperature-controlled incubation equipment
  • Shaker, if used
  • Light microscope and calibrated counting chamber
  • Culture and passage record sheet

2.3 Procedure

  1. Identify the source culture and specify whether the transfer is for routine passaging or expansion.
  2. Assign a separate identifier to every receiving vessel.
  3. Review the source-culture history.
  4. Examine a separate aliquot microscopically.
  5. Record the culture age, appearance, and motility before transfer.
  6. Gently mix the source culture.
  7. Determine its cell density, $C_{\text{source}}$, in cells/mL using the same counting method used for the other cultures in the series.
  8. Define the intended final culture volume, $V_{\text{final}}$, and the intended starting density, $C_{\text{start}}$.
  9. Calculate the required source-culture volume:

$$
V_{\text{source}} =
\frac{C_{\text{start}} \times V_{\text{final}}}
{C_{\text{source}}}
$$

  1. Calculate the required fresh-medium volume:

$$
V_{\text{medium}} =
V_{\text{final}} - V_{\text{source}}
$$

  1. Check the calculation before transfer. If the calculated inoculum exceeds the final volume, dilution alone cannot produce the requested starting density. Resolve the discrepancy using the established culture plan.
  2. Dispense the calculated fresh-medium volume into each labeled receiving vessel.
  3. Gently mix the source culture immediately before withdrawing each inoculum.
  4. Transfer the calculated volume aseptically.
  5. Mix the receiving suspension gently.
  6. For routine passaging, maintain the recorded working volume and culture configuration.
  7. Link the new culture identifier to its parent and update the passage number.
  8. For expansion, distribute the planned total volume among the receiving vessels.
  9. Record the capacity, working volume, and incubation configuration of each vessel.
  10. Incubate under the recorded conditions and document the start time.
  11. Keep separate culture identifiers throughout the expansion process.
  12. At the scheduled assessment, examine the cultures and determine their density.
  13. Record whether each culture meets the documented maintenance or expansion endpoint.
  14. Calculate the cell number represented by each culture when required:

$$
N_{\text{total}} =
C_{\text{final}} \times V_{\text{final}}
$$

where $C_{\text{final}}$ is in cells/mL and $V_{\text{final}}$ is in mL.

  1. Record whether the density measurement represents total cells or viable cells.
  2. Retain individual culture records if material from several vessels is subsequently combined.
  3. Complete the passage record with parent and daughter identifiers, transfer volumes, initial density, incubation conditions, incubation duration, and final density.

3. Tetrahymena Cell Counting

3.1 Purpose

To estimate the concentration of Tetrahymena cells by microscopic enumeration of a known sample volume, with correction for all sample dilution steps.

3.2 Materials

  • Tetrahymena culture with a unique sample identifier
  • Sterile SPP medium for dilution, when used
  • Sterile pipettes, pipette tips, and dilution tubes
  • Calibrated counting chamber and specified coverslip
  • Light microscope with a suitable objective
  • Established sample preparation method for reliable enumeration of motile cells, if required
  • Counting sheet and calculator or spreadsheet

3.3 Procedure

  1. Record the culture identifier and sampling time.
  2. Gently mix the culture to obtain a representative suspension before removing the counting aliquot.
  3. Transfer the aliquot into a labeled tube.
  4. Keep the aliquot separate from the source culture throughout counting.
  5. Prepare the dilution specified by the laboratory method.
  6. Record the culture volume, diluent volume, and final volume at every dilution step.
  7. Mix each dilution before taking material for the next step.
  8. Calculate the total dilution factor:

$$
D = \prod_{i=1}^{n} d_i
$$

where $d_i$ is the dilution factor for each preparation step and $n$ is the number of steps. Include dilution caused by any added counting reagent. For an undiluted sample without reagent addition, $D = 1$.

  1. Apply the established method for obtaining countable cells.
  2. If fixation or another immobilization method is used, apply it only to the counting aliquot and record the reagent, conditions, and dilution contribution.
  3. If cells are counted alive, use a method that prevents movement from causing repeated or missed counts.
  4. Prepare and load the counting chamber according to the manufacturer’s instructions.
  5. Check that the coverslip is correctly positioned and that the chamber is evenly filled.
  6. Reload a chamber with bubbles, leakage, or visibly uneven distribution.
  7. Locate the predefined counting areas.
  8. Focus so that individual Tetrahymena cells can be distinguished from debris.
  9. Apply the same counting-unit definition throughout the dataset.
  10. Count the cells in each selected area.
  11. Use a consistent boundary rule, such as including cells touching the top and left edges and excluding cells touching the bottom and right edges.
  12. Record the raw count for every area.
  13. Determine the total volume counted:

$$
V_{\text{counted}} =
\frac{k \times A \times h}{1000}
\ \text{mL}
$$

where:

  • $k$ is the number of counting areas;
  • $A$ is the area of one counting region in mm²;
  • $h$ is the chamber depth in mm;
  • the factor 1,000 converts mm³ to mL.
  1. Calculate the density of the original culture:

$$
C_{\text{original}} =
\frac{N_{\text{total}} \times D}
{V_{\text{counted}}}
$$

where $N_{\text{total}}$ is the total number of cells counted in all selected areas.

  1. If a counting square is 1 mm × 1 mm and the chamber depth is 0.1 mm, its volume is 0.1 mm³, equivalent to 0.0001 mL. For this geometry:

$$
C_{\text{original}} =
N_{\text{total}} \times D \times 10{,}000
\ \text{cells/mL}
$$

  1. Repeat the measurement using the recorded number of independently loaded chambers.
  2. Calculate a density for each chamber loading before summarizing the results.
  3. Retain the individual density values.
  4. Compare repeat counts with the documented agreement criterion.
  5. If the criterion is not met, review sample mixing, dilution, chamber loading, and counting records before repeating the measurement.
  6. Report the original-culture density in cells/mL together with the raw counts, dilution factor, counted volume, chamber model, sample preparation method, and technical replicate count.
  7. Repeated chamber loadings from one culture are technical measurements, not independent biological replicates.
  8. State the meaning of the reported count. Enumeration of intact cells after fixation represents a total-cell estimate. A viable-cell concentration requires a separately established viability assessment.

4. Pre-experimental Standardization of Tetrahymena Cultures

4.1 Purpose

To prepare Tetrahymena cultures with a documented cultivation history, comparable growth phase, and consistent starting cell density so that differences in initial culture condition can be recognized and controlled.

4.2 Materials

  • Tetrahymena cultures of the same documented species and strain
  • Fresh sterile SPP medium: 10 g/L yeast extract, 1 g/L peptone, and 2 g/L glucose
  • Sterile culture vessels, pipettes, pipette tips, and sampling tubes
  • Temperature-controlled incubation equipment
  • Shaker, if used
  • Light microscope and calibrated counting chamber
  • Existing strain-specific culture records or growth-curve information
  • Sample labels and standardization record sheet

4.3 Procedure

  1. Identify the cultures to be compared.
  2. Record the strain, parent culture, passage history, and medium batch.
  3. Preserve separate identifiers for independently prepared biological replicates.
  4. Prepare pre-cultures using the recorded starting density, working volume, vessel configuration, temperature, and static or shaking conditions.
  5. Record the inoculation time for each culture.
  6. Maintain the documented pre-culture duration and record the actual elapsed time.
  7. If cultures were initiated at different times, retain those differences in the record.
  8. Assess the growth phase using existing observations or growth-curve information for the same strain and culture conditions.
  9. A single density measurement or stated culture age does not by itself establish the growth phase.
  10. Examine a separate aliquot from each culture.
  11. Record morphology, motility, aggregation, and any unusual appearance.
  12. Document cultures that do not meet the inclusion criteria.
  13. Gently mix each culture and measure its density, $C_{\text{measured}}$, using the same counting and sample preparation method across all samples.
  14. Calculate the volume of culture required to prepare the target density, $C_{\text{target}}$, in the intended final suspension volume, $V_{\text{final}}$:

$$
V_{\text{culture}} =
\frac{C_{\text{target}} \times V_{\text{final}}}
{C_{\text{measured}}}
$$

  1. Calculate the required fresh-medium volume:

$$
V_{\text{medium}} =
V_{\text{final}} - V_{\text{culture}}
$$

  1. Combine the calculated culture volume with fresh SPP medium.
  2. Mix gently and retain the link to the source-culture identifier.
  3. Take a separate aliquot from each standardized suspension.
  4. Verify its density using the same counting method.
  5. Record the measured value and compare it with the predefined acceptance range.
  6. Record the final suspension volume and the time of density verification.
  7. If a substantial interval elapses before use, follow the established rule for rechecking density and culture condition.
  8. Gently redistribute the suspension before dispensing aliquots.
  9. Apply the documented allocation and replicate plan while preserving sample identity.
  10. Record the cultivation history, growth-phase evidence, measured starting density, medium batch, final volume, and preparation-to-use interval for each sample.
  11. Describe the preparation accurately in the final Methods text. Comparable culture age and growth phase should not be described as cell-cycle synchronization unless synchronization was independently established.

Auxotrophic Yeast Culture, Selection, Storage, and Growth Monitoring

Protocol ID: P-LLO-06
Prepared by: TDY
Reviewed by: TDY
Version: 2026-10-02
Status: Draft

Source-document note: The DOCX assigns this protocol the identifier P-LLO-05, which duplicates the identifier used for the Tetrahymena culture protocol. It is assigned P-LLO-06 here to maintain unique protocol identifiers.

1. Routine Culture of Auxotrophic W303 Yeast

1.1 Purpose

To culture W303 yeast in liquid or on solid medium for strain maintenance, colony isolation, and experimental use.

1.2 Materials

  • W303 yeast strain
  • YPD liquid medium
  • YPD agar plates
  • SD-Ura or SD-Trp medium when selection is required
  • Sterile culture tubes or baffled flasks
  • Sterile inoculating loops or pipette tips
  • Shaking incubator set to 30°C
  • Plate incubator set to 30°C

1.3 YPD Medium Composition

For 1 L of liquid YPD:

Component Amount Final concentration
Yeast extract 10 g 1% w/v
Peptone 20 g 2% w/v
D-glucose 20 g 2% w/v
Deionized water To 1 L —

For solid YPD, add 20 g of agar per liter.

1.4 YPD Preparation

  1. Dissolve 10 g of yeast extract and 20 g of peptone in approximately 900 mL of deionized water.
  2. For solid medium, add 20 g of agar.
  3. Autoclave at 121°C for 15–20 min.
  4. Cool the medium to approximately 50–60°C.
  5. Add 100 mL of sterile 20% w/v glucose solution.
  6. Mix thoroughly.
  7. For plates, pour approximately 20–25 mL of medium into each Petri dish.
  8. Allow the agar to solidify.

1.5 Liquid Culture Procedure

  1. Transfer one isolated colony into 3–5 mL of liquid medium.
  2. Incubate at 30°C with shaking at 200 rpm for 12–18 h, typically overnight.
  3. For a larger culture, dilute the overnight culture 1:50 to 1:100 into fresh medium.
  4. Continue incubation at 30°C and 200 rpm until the required growth phase is reached.
  5. For log-phase cells, harvest the culture at approximately OD600 0.4–0.8.
  6. Keep the culture volume at or below approximately 20% of the flask volume to maintain sufficient aeration.

1.6 Solid Culture Procedure

  1. Streak yeast cells onto the appropriate agar plate.
  2. Incubate the plate upside down at 30°C.
  3. Incubate YPD plates for approximately 2 days.
  4. Incubate SD-Ura or SD-Trp plates for 2–4 days, depending on colony growth.
  5. Use a well-isolated colony for subsequent liquid culture or strain purification.

2. Preparation of Synthetic Defined Media

2.1 Purpose

To prepare SD-Ura and SD-Trp media for selective culture of W303-derived yeast strains carrying URA3 or TRP1 selection markers.

2.2 Materials

  • SD-Ura powder
  • SD-Trp powder
  • Deionized water
  • Sterile 40% w/v glucose solution
  • Agar, for solid medium
  • Autoclave
  • Sterile bottles
  • Sterile Petri dishes

2.3 SD-Ura Medium

For approximately 1 L:

Component Amount
SD-Ura powder 8 g
Deionized water 950 mL
Sterile 40% glucose 50 mL
Agar, for solid medium 20 g

The final glucose concentration is 2% w/v.

2.4 SD-Trp Medium

For approximately 1 L:

Component Amount
SD-Trp powder 8 g
Deionized water 950 mL
Sterile 40% glucose 50 mL
Agar, for solid medium 20 g

The final glucose concentration is 2% w/v.

2.5 Preparation of 40% Glucose Solution

  1. Dissolve 400 g of D-glucose in deionized water.
  2. Adjust the final volume to 1 L.
  3. Sterilize separately by filtration through a 0.22 μm filter.
  4. Store the sterile solution at 4°C in a clean, tightly closed bottle.

2.6 SD-Ura Preparation

  1. Add 8 g of SD-Ura powder to 950 mL of deionized water.
  2. Mix until the powder has dissolved completely.
  3. For SD-Ura agar, add 20 g of agar before autoclaving.
  4. Autoclave at 121°C for 15–20 min.
  5. Cool the medium to approximately 50–60°C.
  6. Aseptically add 50 mL of sterile 40% glucose.
  7. Mix thoroughly without introducing excessive bubbles.
  8. For liquid medium, dispense into sterile bottles or culture containers.
  9. For solid medium, pour approximately 20–25 mL into each Petri dish.
  10. Allow the agar to solidify.
  11. Store the medium at 4°C.

2.7 SD-Trp Preparation

  1. Add 8 g of SD-Trp powder to 950 mL of deionized water.
  2. Mix until the powder has dissolved completely.
  3. For SD-Trp agar, add 20 g of agar before autoclaving.
  4. Autoclave at 121°C for 15–20 min.
  5. Cool the medium to approximately 50–60°C.
  6. Aseptically add 50 mL of sterile 40% glucose.
  7. Mix thoroughly.
  8. Dispense the liquid medium or pour agar plates as required.

Source consistency note: The source DOCX lists sterile 20% glucose in one SD-Trp preparation step, while the formulation uses 50 mL of glucose solution in 950 mL of medium and specifies a final glucose concentration of 2%. The organized version therefore uses sterile 40% glucose consistently.

3. Yeast Selection and Single-Colony Expansion

3.1 Purpose

To select transformed yeast, isolate clonal transformants, expand individual colonies, and maintain selection-dependent phenotypes.

3.2 Materials

  • Transformed W303 yeast cells
  • SD-Ura agar and liquid medium
  • SD-Trp agar and liquid medium
  • YPD medium for optional post-transformation recovery
  • Sterile water
  • Sterile spreaders
  • Sterile inoculating loops or pipette tips
  • Sterile culture tubes and flasks
  • Incubator and shaking incubator set to 30°C

3.3 Post-Transformation Selection

  1. Resuspend the cells in 200–500 μL of sterile water or recovery medium.

  2. If recovery is required, incubate the cells in YPD at 30°C with shaking at 200 rpm for approximately 1 h.

  3. Spread 50–200 μL of the cell suspension onto the appropriate selective plate.

  4. If low transformation efficiency is expected, plate the remaining cells on a second selective plate.

  5. Prepare the following controls when needed:

    • Untransformed cells on the selective plate as a negative control
    • Untransformed cells on YPD as a viability control

  6. Incubate the plates upside down at 30°C for 2–4 days.

  7. Do not interpret very small colonies or background growth until colonies are clearly distinguishable.

3.4 Single-Colony Isolation

  1. Select a well-separated colony from the transformation plate.
  2. Streak the colony onto a fresh plate containing the same selective medium.
  3. Incubate at 30°C for 2–3 days.
  4. Confirm that individual colonies have formed along the final streak area.
  5. Pick one isolated colony for liquid expansion.

Restreaking before expansion reduces the chance of maintaining mixed transformants or neighboring background colonies.

3.5 Liquid Expansion

  1. Inoculate one isolated colony into 3–5 mL of the appropriate selective liquid medium.
  2. Incubate at 30°C and 200 rpm for 12–18 h.
  3. For larger-scale culture, dilute the overnight culture 1:50 to 1:100 into fresh selective medium.
  4. Incubate at 30°C and 200 rpm.
  5. Harvest during log phase, typically at OD600 0.4–0.8, when actively growing cells are required.
  6. Use part of the confirmed culture to prepare a glycerol stock.

3.6 Maintenance of Phenotypic Stability

  1. Use YPD only when non-selective recovery or rapid biomass production is specifically required.
  2. Avoid repeated overnight passaging and prolonged stationary-phase incubation.
  3. Prepare a master glycerol stock shortly after clone verification.
  4. Start new experiments from the master stock or from a limited-passage working stock.
  5. Restreak strains on selective agar before critical experiments.
  6. Verify the expected phenotype, plasmid, or DNA integration after recovery when experimental conclusions depend on genotype stability.

4. Yeast Glycerol Stock Preparation and Recovery

4.1 Purpose

To preserve W303 yeast strains at −80°C and recover viable clonal cultures while maintaining the required selection marker.

4.2 Materials

  • Fresh overnight yeast culture
  • Sterile 30% v/v glycerol solution
  • Sterile cryovials
  • Appropriate selective agar plates
  • YPD agar plates, when selection is not required
  • −80°C freezer
  • Sterile inoculating loops or pipette tips

4.3 Freezing Solution

Mixing equal volumes of yeast culture and sterile 30% glycerol produces a final glycerol concentration of 15%.

4.4 Glycerol Stock Preparation

  1. Grow a confirmed single-colony culture overnight in 3–5 mL of the appropriate medium.
  2. Label a sterile cryovial with the strain name, selection marker, clone number, and date.
  3. Add 500 μL of overnight yeast culture to the cryovial.
  4. Add 500 μL of sterile 30% glycerol.
  5. Mix thoroughly by gentle inversion or pipetting. Avoid vigorous vortexing.
  6. Place the cryovial directly at −80°C.
  7. Keep a master stock and a separate working stock when the strain will be used frequently.
  8. Do not use −20°C for long-term yeast storage.

4.5 Recovery

  1. Remove the glycerol stock from the −80°C freezer.
  2. Keep the vial cold and avoid thawing the entire stock.
  3. Use a sterile loop or pipette tip to scrape a small amount of frozen material from the surface.
  4. Immediately return the vial to −80°C.
  5. Streak the collected cells onto the appropriate agar plate.
  6. Incubate at 30°C for 2–4 days.
  7. Pick one isolated colony into 3–5 mL of the corresponding liquid medium.
  8. Incubate at 30°C and 200 rpm for 12–18 h.
  9. Reconfirm the expected phenotype or genotype before critical experiments.

5. Monitoring Yeast Growth

5.1 Purpose

To quantify yeast growth by OD600, generate growth curves, and compare growth under different media or selection conditions.

5.2 Materials

  • Yeast cultures
  • YPD, SD-Ura, or SD-Trp medium
  • Spectrophotometer or microplate reader capable of measuring OD600
  • Cuvettes or clear microplates
  • Sterile culture tubes or flasks
  • Sterile medium for dilution
  • Shaking incubator set to 30°C
  • Data analysis or graphing software

5.3 OD600 Measurement

  1. Turn on the spectrophotometer and select a wavelength of 600 nm.
  2. Use uninoculated medium as the blank.
  3. Use the same medium for blanking and sample preparation. For example, blank SD-Ura samples with sterile SD-Ura medium.
  4. Mix the yeast culture thoroughly before sampling because yeast cells settle rapidly.
  5. Transfer the required volume to a clean cuvette.
  6. Measure and record the OD600.
  7. Measure samples within the linear range of the instrument. A practical range is approximately OD600 0.1–0.8, although the exact range depends on the instrument.
  8. Dilute samples with the same sterile medium when the OD600 is above the linear range.

5.4 Dilution Procedure

For a 1:10 dilution:

  1. Add 100 μL of yeast culture to 900 μL of sterile medium.
  2. Mix thoroughly.
  3. Measure the diluted sample at OD600.
  4. Calculate the original OD600:

$$
\text{OD}{600,\text{original}} =
\text{OD}{600,\text{measured}}
\times
\text{dilution factor}
$$

Example:

  • Measured OD600: 0.65
  • Dilution factor: 10
  • Original OD600: 6.5
Dilution Culture volume Medium volume Dilution factor
1:2 500 μL 500 μL 2
1:5 200 μL 800 μL 5
1:10 100 μL 900 μL 10
1:20 50 μL 950 μL 20

5.5 Growth Curve Procedure

  1. Start from freshly isolated colonies or equivalent overnight cultures.
  2. Prepare at least three independent biological replicates for each condition.
  3. Grow overnight cultures in the medium appropriate for each strain.
  4. Measure the OD600 of each overnight culture.
  5. Dilute each culture into fresh medium to the same starting OD600, typically 0.05.
  6. Use identical culture volumes, flask sizes, temperatures, and shaking speeds for all conditions.
  7. Incubate at 30°C and 200 rpm.
  8. Record OD600 at time zero.
  9. Measure OD600 every 1–2 h during active growth.
  10. Continue measurements for 12–24 h or until the cultures reach stationary phase.
  11. Dilute samples when necessary and correct each reading using the dilution factor.
  12. Plot corrected OD600 against time.

5.6 Growth Rate Calculation

Select two time points within the exponential growth phase.

Calculate the specific growth rate:

$$
\mu =
\frac{\ln(\text{OD}{2})-\ln(\text{OD}{1})}
{t_{2}-t_{1}}
$$

Calculate the doubling time:

$$
t_{\text{doubling}} =
\frac{\ln 2}{\mu}
$$

Use only exponential-phase measurements for these calculations.

5.7 Comparison of Growth Conditions

When comparing YPD, SD-Ura, and SD-Trp cultures:

  1. Use the same strain or a clearly defined genotype for each comparison.
  2. Start all cultures at the same OD600.
  3. Maintain the same temperature, shaking speed, flask type, and culture-to-flask volume ratio.
  4. Blank each sample against its own uninoculated medium.
  5. Use at least three biological replicates.
